The question everyone asks

How to build immunity

There is no monthly course that makes a bird immune. Here is what the research says, and what to do instead.

First, the correction. Antibodies are made by the bird's own immune system when it meets an antigen, through vaccination or real exposure. No supplement creates an antibody. What nutrition decides is how well the bird can mount that response. Correcting a deficiency raises antibody output sharply. Megadosing a bird that is already adequate does not, and fat soluble vitamins accumulate.
Vitamin E raises antibody output
Supplemented birds produced antibodies earlier and in greater quantity after vaccination. Deficient birds showed depressed production
Vitamin A deficiency blocks it
Deficiency reduces the ability to synthesise specific antibodies and weakens lymphocyte response
Vitamin C helps under stress
Improves antibody mediated response specifically in birds under stress conditions
Ranked by real effect

In this order. Bottles are third, and they only matter as insurance against deficiency.

Biosecurity

The single biggest factor. Four week quarantine for every new bird, separate room, handled last every day. No contact with pigeons or wild birds. Most outbreaks walk in on a new bird or on your own hands.

02
DAILY ROUTINE
15 minutes
Tray paper · wipe dry · soft food out in 2 hours

Dry hygiene

Change tray paper daily and wipe trays dry. Remove uneaten soft food within two hours. Dry every drinker daily. Trichomonas dies when dry. Most bacterial load comes from wet droppings, not from air.

Nutritional adequacy

The goal is no deficiency, not maximum dose. A complete daily multivitamin plus a proper seed and soft food base covers all of them. More does not mean stronger.

04
PROBIOTIC

Gut flora

Good bacteria physically occupy the gut so pathogens cannot colonise. Five days at the start of every month, and five days after any antibiotic course. The one routine monthly course that is genuinely supported.

05
KEEP BELOW
32°C
calm room · steady light · no sudden noise

Low stress

Heat above 32°C, crowding, constant disturbance and sudden noise all raise stress hormones, and stress hormones suppress immune function directly. A calm, ventilated, cool room does more than any bottle.

06
REAL ANTIBODIES
The only one
on this entire list

Vaccination

Where a vaccine exists for a disease in your region, such as pox, it is the only item on this list that creates real antibodies. Ask an avian vet what is available locally.

Why a monthly 7 day course is a bad idea

  • Antibiotics and antiprotozoals given routinely breed resistance. When you actually need the drug for a sick bird, it no longer works
  • They kill gut flora, which removes the protection described in rank 04 above
  • They mask early symptoms, so you find disease later and sicker
  • A healthy flock on a monthly antimicrobial is being treated for nothing
If someone recommends a product you cannot verify. Ask for the manufacturer's own page, the composition, and the dose per litre. If none of those exist in writing, do not put it in your birds on a monthly schedule. An unverifiable product run twelve times a year is twelve chances to cause harm you cannot diagnose.
The honest summary. If you already run a daily multivitamin, a monthly probiotic, proper quarantine, dry trays and a cool ventilated room, you are already running the best immunity programme available. There is nothing to add. The next gain is not another product, it is doing these six consistently.

Sources: Effects of vitamins A, D, E and C on the chicken immune system, Poultry Science · Vitamin A deficiency impairs mucosal immune function in chicks, PMC

Read before any dose

Three rules that prevent most harm

Most supplement damage in an aviary comes from stacking and from timing, not from underdosing.

Rule 1 · Never combine on the same day

  • Two fertility products, such as Fetura 12 with Ferti-Vit, Nekton E or Nekton Breed Star
  • Two vitamin D3 products. Calciform, Calcivet, Avigold Advance, Breed Star and A21 all carry D3
  • Two electrolyte products, such as Spark Liquid with Nekton Elektrolyt or Prolyte-C
  • Any probiotic during a course of medication
Rule 2 · Stop at the first egg. Nekton E and Nekton Breed Star are pre breeding products. Nekton's own label says to stop the day the first egg is laid, because continuing drives the male too hard and disrupts incubation. Running them through incubation is a known way to lose a clutch.
Rule 3 · The water rule. Medicated water must be the only water source, mixed fresh every day, and left in the cage no longer than six hours, or three hours on a hot afternoon. Never withhold drinking water overnight to force feeding, that risks dehydration and egg binding.
Why D3 stacking matters. Vitamin D3 is fat soluble and accumulates. Chronic excess causes calcium deposits in soft tissue including the kidneys. At label rate a high strength liquid D3 product can carry four times the D3 of a calcium product, so that is the one which must never be doubled.

Timing

The feeding system

Timing decides whether birds actually eat the supplements you paid for.

5:00 PM · Seed out

Seed pot out. Plain water stays in the cage all night. Never withhold water, a 30°C room plus a laying hen is an egg binding risk.

6:00 AM · The switch

Plain water out. Medicated water in. Soft food in. Birds are hungry and thirsty at the same moment, so they take both. This is how supplements actually get eaten.

Within 2 hours · Soft food out

Out whether finished or not. One hour in hot or wet weather. Boiled egg at 30°C in high humidity is a bacterial culture medium.

Max 6 hours · Water refreshed

Medicated water out and replaced. Three hours on a hot afternoon.

Evening · Cycle repeats

Seed pot back in, fresh.

Soft food quantities. Non breeding pairs: one boiled egg, a half spoon of rearing formula, herbs. Breeding pairs: two eggs, twice daily. Resting hens: two eggs per week only, no rearing formula, or she comes back into condition too early.
The room

Environment targets

Nutrition decides fertility. Environment decides hatch. These are the numbers that matter.

Temperature
Keep below 32°C. Above that, poultry research shows damage to sperm producing cells and falling testosterone. Heat sterility in males is real and reversible
Humidity
Target VPD 9 to 15 hPa during incubation, not a raw humidity percentage. VPD accounts for temperature, raw RH does not
Ventilation
10 to 15 air changes per hour, CO2 under 3,000 ppm. A fan can only bring indoor humidity down to outdoor humidity, never below it
Ammonia
Your nose detects it at about 5 ppm. If you smell it at 6 AM the answer is daily dry trays, not a longer fan cycle. Ammonia comes from wet droppings, not from air
Light
12 to 14 hours, consistent. Set lights to bracket the natural day so the total stays constant as daylight shortens. Do not extend for more activity, it over drives hens
UVB
Glass blocks 100% of UVB. Indoor birds make zero natural vitamin D3. Either a UVB lamp or a dietary D3 product, one or the other, not neither
Nest box
Box humidity always runs above room humidity because of the hen breathing in a sealed cavity. Six to eight small vent holes near the top. Thin dry nesting. No bath water during incubation

Breeding window

The fertility sequence

Two products, never on the same day. They act on different sperm, so the order and the timing decide whether they work at all.

Day minus 14 to minus 1 · Ferti-Vit

4 g per litre of water, daily. Builds one complete sperm cycle under vitamin E, selenium and zinc. Rooster spermatogenesis takes 14 days, so this window cannot be shortened. Ferti-Vit is the only product here that declares selenium. No calcium and D3 product on these days, it already carries 728 IU D3 per litre.

Day 0 · Box goes in, switch to Fetura 12

2 ml per litre of water. Stop Ferti-Vit the same day. L-carnitine at 100 mg per litre acts on sperm that already exist, improving motility and viability, so it works in days where vitamin E needs a fortnight.

Day 1 to 15 · Fetura continues

Covers the whole mating window. Mating usually starts day 9 or 10, so the male must already be primed when it does.

First egg · Stop both

Versele and Nekton both state this on their own labels. Switch to the calcium and D3 product through laying.

Why a low rating does not mean a weak product. Fetura 12 scores 2.2 out of 10 on a fertility comparison table because it is not a vitamin product. It carries no vitamin A, no D3, no selenium. What it carries is the only L-carnitine in the aviary, and that is the only lever that can move an egg laid next week. Ferti-Vit scores 6.5 and cannot. Match the product to the week, not to the score.

The rescue shelf

None of these run on a schedule. Each one has a trigger. If the trigger is not present, the bottle stays closed.

Fetura 12 · a pair came back with clear eggs

2 ml per litre, day 0 to +15 of the next box, instead of Ferti-Vit. L-carnitine 100 mg per litre acts on sperm that already exist, so it works in days where vitamin E needs fourteen. Also for any male six weeks without a fertile egg. Stop at the first egg.

Calcivet by syringe · egg bound or leg paralysed hen

0.2 ml per 100 g body weight into the beak. Labelled for direct dosing. Then warm quiet cage, perch out, food and water at floor level. Never on a Calciform day.

Nekton B-Komplex · heavy laying, leg weakness, appetite loss

0.5 g per small bird per day in soft food, 5 days. That is Nekton's label. For a 500 g batch feeding about 40 birds, 20 g. Folate 6 mg per gram, the richest source you own. Then back to the phase mix.

Dirovet · canker, on diagnosis

40 mg per kg once daily, 5 days. A 50 g lovebird is 6.7 mg of powder. Metronidazole 300 mg per gram. Micronid does not treat canker, it is an antibiotic.

Six Plus · damp seed, a mould smell, monsoon

1 ml per 4 litres, 3 to 5 days. Yeast binder for mycotoxin plus seven acids to drop water pH. Contains copper: never with ACV the same day, never with Two-Plus.

Elektrolyt or Prolyte-C · heat day, transport, any bird off colour

Elektrolyt 7.5 g per litre, maximum 5 days. Prolyte-C 10 g per litre once it arrives, and it adds 3 billion live bacteria. Not during a medication course, only after.

Mycoform-CA · sneezing, dust, after transport

10 ml per litre, 5 days. Respiratory support. Once it arrives.

Avigold · quarantine, new arrivals, any bird not eating soft food

10 ml per litre. The liquid multivitamin for a bird that cannot get the soft food mix. Not part of the daily system.

Micronid · vet confirmed bacterial infection only

1 g per litre, 3 to 5 days. Erythromycin, sulfadiazine, trimethoprim. Never a routine course. Sulfadiazine carries a kidney risk in small birds.

Leg paralysis in a laying hen

Two hens went down last season and recovered on calcium and B vitamins. Recovery on nutrition points to a metabolic cause, not injury.

Running 56 cages

The rotation, run professionally

One male, two females, nine clutches per male unit in a season. The rules that keep a 90% male at 90%.

One card per cage

Pair ID, clutch number, date boxed, date of first egg, egg count, which foster took them, hatch result, which phase the pair is in. A season without cards is a season you cannot learn from.

Plan the foster before you box the female

The foster pair is the true bottleneck. Never install a box unless you already know which foster takes that clutch.

Stagger box installation 3 to 4 days apart

If ten females lay in the same week you need ten fosters in the same week, and you do not have ten spare.

Never run both of a male's females at once

Female 2 starts while female 1 rests. His rest is built into the alternation. Breaking it is how a 90% male becomes a 60% male.

The male never moves

Females travel to his cage. Moving a male resets territory behaviour and costs days.

Keel check at every transfer, both directions

Two seconds by hand. Round means ready, sharp means give her longer. It is the only thing between a productive hen and cage layer fatigue.

Weigh eggs from day one, open every dead egg

Target 13 to 16% weight loss by day 21. The stage of death is the diagnosis, and by December you will know your exact enemy instead of guessing.

Rare mutation eggs go to your best proven foster

Never test an unproven foster on a dun fallow or bronze fallow clutch.

Review the numbers monthly, not at season end

Fertility, hatch, fledge per pair. A pair failing twice is data. A pair failing five times is a decision you delayed.

The 5th floor rule. August and September, outdoor humidity 83 to 90%: an open balcony sits at outdoor humidity and cannot be ventilated below it. Do not breed valuable pairs there before November. October to March, 76% falling to 61%: excellent, open air, natural light. That is what 5F is for. Shade cloth on the sun facing side regardless.
New birds

Quarantine, four weeks, no shortcuts

Imported and newly bought birds arrive dehydrated, immune suppressed and often carrying something latent that flares under transport stress.

Week 1 · Settle only

Separate room. Electrolyte and rehydration only. Warmth. Minimal handling. Let the bird eat, drink and settle.

Week 2 · Build condition

Add a daily multivitamin and light soft food. Watch droppings, breathing and weight daily.

Weeks 3 and 4 · Treat what you saw

Any treatment course indicated by what you have actually observed, not by routine. Probiotic five days afterwards. For canker use metronidazole (Dirovet), not Micronid.

Every day of all four weeks

Handle quarantine birds last. Wash hands and change footwear before returning to the main flock.

Never

  • Move a new bird into breeding before four weeks are complete
  • Handle quarantine birds before the main flock
  • Share bowls, perches or tools between quarantine and the flock
  • Treat canker with Micronid. It is an antibiotic (erythromycin, sulfadiazine, trimethoprim), not an antiprotozoal. Metronidazole is the canker drug
If several new birds die across separate purchases from different sellers, the common factor is not the sellers. It is the receiving process. That is a protocol problem with a known fix, not bad luck.

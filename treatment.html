The one that catches people: vitamin E and selenium are a pair, not a sequence. Breed Star carries the vitamin E and no selenium. Ferti-Vit carries both. That is why the breeding rotation is 2 days and 2 days, and why a 7-day block of either one on its own wastes half of it.

Act within the hour. Five situations that decide whether a bird recovers or is lost. Learn these before you need them.

CorrectedTwo lines in the old heat card were wrong and have been replaced. The old text said to wet the floor above 35 °C and to run every fan at once. Wetting the floor raises humidity, and a bird cools itself only by panting, which stops working as the air fills. At 34 °C, lifting relative humidity from 72 % to 88 % collapses vapour pressure deficit from 14.9 hPa to 6.4 hPa. And the 6F exhaust between 10:00 and 14:00 pulls in outdoor air measured 2.0 to 2.4 °C hotter than the room, so running it at midday imports heat. Ceiling fans yes. Exhaust at midday, no.

Leg paralysis

Quiet cage, perches removed, food and water at floor level. Liquid calcium by syringe into the beak, repeated after two hours. B complex, electrolyte for hydration, warmth and no disturbance. Most laying related cases resolve within two to three days once calcium is restored.

On your shelf: Calcivet 0.2 ml per 100 g body weight by syringe, then Nekton B-Komplex 0.5 g in the daily ration.

Weak or fluffed bird

Warmth first, always. A cold bird cannot recover no matter what you give it. Then electrolyte solution in water and warm boiled egg mash. If there is no improvement within two days, isolate the bird and reassess.

Never withhold water from a weak or underweight bird to force it onto medicated water. That technique is for healthy birds only.

Loose droppings

Stop every supplement for 24 hours and give plain water with a little vinegar. From day two, run a probiotic for five days. Electrolyte for a maximum of five days. If droppings are bloody or continue past two days, escalate to a vet.

Never stack acidifiers. PB-71 already contains probiotics and acidifiers, so it must not share a day with Synbiotic, vinegar, Two-Plus or Six Plus.

Heat emergency

Every ceiling fan to full, aimed at bird level, not at the nest boxes. Check the boxes for hens off their eggs. Ice in the drinkers. Mist the panting birds directly, or put out bath dishes. Electrolyte in the drinkers for the rest of the day.

Never wet the floor, and never run the exhaust between 10:00 and 14:00. Both make the room worse. The exhaust window is 20:00 to 07:00, when outdoor air is 1.7 to 3.1 °C cooler than the room.

A new bird arrives

Four weeks of quarantine, in a separate room, handled last every day. Week one is electrolyte only. Week two adds a daily tonic and light soft food. Weeks three and four cover the quarantine medication course. Never shorten it.

Separate airspace, separate bowls, separate tools, wash hands after. A visible keel after transport is weight loss, not illness. No Breed Star or Ferti-Vit until quarantine ends and weight is normal.

#3 Super cold and hapani
protocol from GK Fuad		 Heavy cold with laboured breathing. Hapani is the wheeze you can hear across the room Doxy 1 g + Electromin 0.5 g in 250 ml, 5 days
Then 5 days of probiotic, vitamin and mineral supplement, and protein		 Recovered. DROPPINGS
#4 Chuna or green droppings White chalky or green droppings. Green means bile with no food passing. Chalky white means urates without solids Micronid or Ciprocin Both are antibacterials, so both target a bacterial enteritis. That is a reasonable first guess for green droppings in a bird that is otherwise eating Micronid has no published dose. The pattern across all four: every case that recovered was treated for an infection, not a deficiency, and the vitamins were support rather than the cure. That is worth remembering when a bird goes quiet. The one change worth making next season is to stop putting Electromin or any mineral in the same water as doxycycline or ciprofloxacin.

The weekly check loop

Five trackers, one order, one pass. Run it the same time every week so a problem is caught by measurement instead of by a dead bird.

Step 1

Treatment Tracker

Anything overdue is stopped today. Anything finishing today is the next course scheduled. Five days is the hard ceiling on every electrolyte.

Step 2

Weather and VPD

Export the week from eWeLink. Mean vapour pressure deficit, hours in the 9 to 15 hPa band, dew point gap, hours above 32 °C. Above a 1.5 °C gap the problem is airflow, near zero it is the weather.

Step 3

eWeLink rule audit

Open Timer Management and read every schedule and every Auto list. A forgotten rule once switched the Room 2 exhaust off all afternoon for weeks. Look for rules you did not create.

Step 4

Egg weight log

Every clutch, every third day. 13 to 16 % loss by day 21, about 0.6 to 0.8 % a day. This is the only test that separates humidity from every other cause of dead in shell.

Step 5

Season and shopping

Which phase every pair is in, and what runs out inside three weeks. Ordering after a bottle is empty costs a breeding window, not just money.

Step 6

Medicine Tracker

Only when something changes: a new product, a new label, a dose you are unsure of. Every verified figure links to the manufacturer page it came from.

Never combine on the same day

  • Two fertility products, such as Fetura 12 with Fertivit or Nekton E
  • Two vitamin D3 products, such as Calciform with Calcivet, MSA or A21
  • Two electrolyte products, such as Spark Liquid with Nekton Elektrolyt
  • Any probiotic during a course of medication
Water rule. Medicated water must be the only water source, mixed fresh every day, and left in the cage no longer than six hours, or three hours on a hot afternoon. All doses below are set for lovebirds, which sit at the low end of most parrot labels.

Act within the hour

Emergency actions

Five situations that decide whether a bird recovers or is lost. Learn these before you need them.

Leg paralysis

Quiet cage, perches removed, food and water at floor level. Liquid calcium by syringe into the beak, repeated after two hours. B complex in water, electrolyte for hydration, warmth and no disturbance. Most laying related cases resolve within two to three days once calcium is restored.

Weak or fluffed bird

Warmth first, always. A cold bird cannot recover no matter what you give it. Then electrolyte solution in water and warm boiled egg mash. If there is no improvement within two days, isolate the bird and reassess.

Loose droppings

Stop every supplement for 24 hours and give plain water with a little vinegar. From day two, run a probiotic for five days. If droppings are bloody or continue past two days, escalate to a vet.

Heat emergency

Run every fan at once, then check nest boxes for hens off their eggs. Above 35 degrees, wet the floor for evaporative cooling. Electrolyte solution in the drinkers for the rest of the day.

A new bird arrives

Four weeks of quarantine, in a separate room, handled last every day. Week one is electrolyte only. Week two adds a daily tonic and light soft food. Weeks three and four cover the quarantine medication course. Never shorten it.

This page is a reference tool, not veterinary advice. Doses are taken from manufacturer labels and adjusted toward the low end of published ranges because lovebirds are small birds. Always read the label on your own bottle, since formulations change, and consult an avian veterinarian for a sick bird. Medication should never be given to a healthy flock without a diagnosis.
